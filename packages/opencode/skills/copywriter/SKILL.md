Use established copywriting frameworks and brand-specific adaptation to produce persuasive marketing copy.

## AIDA Framework
- **Attention**: Hook in the first line - bold statement, provocative question, surprising statistic, or relatable pain point.
- **Interest**: Build curiosity by detailing the problem or opportunity. Use specific details, storytelling, or data.
- **Desire**: Make the reader want the outcome. Paint a vivid picture of the benefit, show social proof, or highlight scarcity.
- **Action**: Clear, single-focus CTA. Use imperative verbs, low-friction phrasing, and reiterate the value of acting now.

## Benefit-Driven Writing
- Lead with benefits, not features. For every feature listed, ask "so what?" and write the benefit.
- Use "you" and "your" more than "we" and "our" - the customer is the hero.
- Quantify outcomes where possible ("3x faster", "save 10 hours/week").
- Address objections preemptively: "You might be wondering about X - here's how we handle it."

## CTAs
- Use action-oriented verbs: Start, Get, Build, Unlock, Launch, Claim, Join.
- Include a specific value proposition within or adjacent to the CTA ("Get the Playbook", not just "Download").
- Create urgency or FOMO when appropriate: limited time, limited spots, exclusive access.
- Avoid generic CTAs ("Submit", "Click Here") - every CTA should convey the benefit of clicking.

## Headline Formulas
- **How-to**: "How to [achieve desired outcome] without [pain point]"
- **List**: "[Number] [adjective] ways to [benefit]"
- **Question**: "Why [common belief] is ruining your [goal]"
- **Command**: "[Verb] your [noun] with [unique approach]"
- **Curiosity gap**: "What [industry leaders] know about [topic] that you don't"

## Adapting Tone to Medium
- **Landing pages**: Benefit-forward, scannable, emotional hooks, social proof.
- **Emails**: Conversational, personal ("I"/"you"), shorter paragraphs, single CTA.
- **Ads**: Minimal copy, maximal impact. One strong hook, one clear benefit, one CTA.
- **Social media**: Platform-native cadence. Short lines for mobile, hooks in first 2-3 words.
